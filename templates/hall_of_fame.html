Club History

Arsenal Football Club was founded in 1886 by workers at the Royal Arsenal munitions factory in Woolwich, South London. Originally named Dial Square, it became Woolwich Arsenal and, in 1893, was the first southern club to join the Football League. Financial struggles and poor crowds led to a pivotal change in 1913, when the club moved to Highbury in North London and soon dropped “Woolwich” from its name.

In 1925, Arsenal hired visionary manager Herbert Chapman, who transformed the club with new tactics, numbered shirts, and the iconic white sleeves. Under Chapman and his successors, Arsenal became a dominant force in the 1930s, winning multiple league titles and FA Cups. A league and FA Cup double followed in 1971 under Bertie Mee, re-establishing Arsenal as one of England’s top clubs.

The modern era was defined by Arsène Wenger, who arrived in 1996 and revolutionized English football with new approaches to fitness, diet, and scouting. He led the club to three Premier League titles, including the unbeaten “Invincibles” season of 2003–04, and won a record seven FA Cups. In 2006, Arsenal moved from Highbury to the Emirates Stadium to secure its financial future.

After a transitional period following Wenger’s departure in 2018, Mikel Arteta has rebuilt the team around young talent and a clear tactical identity. Arsenal remains one of England’s most successful clubs, with 13 league titles and a record 14 FA Cups, known for its stylish football and rich history.

Golden Era: The Invincible Team

The 2003-04 season was undoubtedly one of the most brilliant periods in Arsenal's history. Under Arsene Wenger, the Gunners managed to finish the entire Premier League season unbeaten, earning them the nickname "The Invincibles".

With players such as Thierry Henry, Dennis Bergkamp, ​​Patrick Vieira, Robert Pearce and Ashley Cole, the team won 26 and drew 12, winning the Premier League with 90 points.
